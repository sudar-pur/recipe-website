Hey, glad you're here.

I'm a lifelong vegetarian who got tired of hearing that you can't build real strength without eating meat.

I grew up in an Indian household eating incredibly well — dal and rice on weeknights, paneer at restaurants on weekends, chana masala, stuffed parathas, sambar with idli, the works. I had great exposure to all kinds of vegetarian food. I never felt like I was missing out on anything. But when I started lifting in college, suddenly protein became the only thing anyone talked about, and every conversation led to the same answer: eat more chicken. Every meal plan, every fitness influencer, every gym bro — chicken breast on repeat. And there I was, a vegetarian, being told my diet was fundamentally broken.

So I started measuring. I tracked everything. Turns out the food I'd been eating my whole life was already packed with protein — I just never thought about it that way. A cup of dal has 18g. Paneer is basically a protein brick. Two eggs at breakfast and a bowl of rajma for dinner and you're already halfway there. I started being more intentional about it — adding cottage cheese into curries, swapping cream for Greek yogurt, doubling the chickpeas — and I started hitting my targets. 140, 150, 160 grams a day. All vegetarian. All actually delicious.

Why Deep Roots?

I built this site because the resource I needed didn't exist. Vegetarian recipe blogs don't focus on protein. High-protein meal plans assume you eat meat. If you're vegetarian and trying to build muscle, you're left to figure it out on your own.

Every recipe here is designed with protein as a primary consideration — not an afterthought. I list the protein per serving prominently, include tips for boosting it further, and make sure every dish is something you'd actually want to eat, not just tolerate for the macros.

A lot of the recipes here start from what I grew up eating — Indian dals, paneer dishes, egg curries — but I've also pulled from cuisines I fell in love with along the way: Mediterranean lentil bowls, Mexican bean enchiladas, Asian tofu stir fries. If it's vegetarian, high in protein, and actually good, it belongs here.

What I Believe

Protein First

Every recipe starts with a protein target. Flavor is non-negotiable, but protein is the foundation. If a dish doesn't deliver meaningful protein, it doesn't make it onto this site.

Real Food

No protein powders in my recipes (though I have nothing against them). Everything here is built from whole, recognizable ingredients — lentils, beans, yogurt, paneer, eggs, tofu.

Built to Last

These aren't trendy recipes that'll disappear next month. Lentils, beans, and yogurt have been fueling strong people for thousands of years. I'm just putting the macros on them.

Keep It Simple

I'm not a professional chef. I'm a guy who goes to the gym and needs to eat well afterward. These recipes are designed for real kitchens, real schedules, and real appetites.

The Numbers

Since I started eating this way, I've maintained a consistent protein intake of 140-160g per day on a fully vegetarian diet. I've put on 25 pounds of lean mass over the past four years. My lifts have gone up steadily.

I'm not saying this to brag — I'm saying it because I spent years thinking it wasn't possible. It is. You just need the right recipes.

Welcome to Deep Roots. Let's cook.

— The Deep Roots Kitchen
